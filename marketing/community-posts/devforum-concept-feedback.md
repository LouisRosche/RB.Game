## Post Body

Hey DevForum,

I've been building a new idle/simulator and would love some honest feedback
before I commit to the full build.

**The concept: Alchemy Academy**

Genre: idle simulator in the "Grow a ___" format (Grow a Garden, etc.)
Twist: every potion recipe unlocks a real science fact (bioluminescence,
nuclear fusion, geothermal energy) displayed in an in-game Alchemist's Journal.

**Core loop:**
1. Buy ingredient seeds (4 ingredients: Moonpetal, Fireroot, Shadowmoss, Starbloom)
2. Plant > wait > harvest (mutation rarity rolled at harvest: Common to Legendary, 5 tiers)
3. Combine 2 ingredients at cauldron > discover one of 10 recipes > sell for gold
4. Upgrade lab (2>4>6 plots) and cauldron (Basic>Copper>Silver)
5. Repeat — trying for higher rarity mutations (1% Legendary chance, shown transparently)

**What makes it different from other "Grow a" games:**

- **Discovery mechanic**: "What does this combo make?" adds exploration beyond pure idle waiting
- **Transparent odds**: full probability table shown in-game. No mystery. No loot-box feeling.
- **Educational hook**: the science angle gives parents and streamers a reason to engage beyond "Roblox idle game"
- **Anti-dark-pattern design**: no FOMO timers, no guilt notifications, plants wait indefinitely

**Monetization plan (Phase 2):**
Cosmetics only — cauldron skins, lab decorations, potion bottle styles. No pay-to-win, no game pass required for progression.

**Questions for the community:**

1. Does the "science + idle" angle feel differentiated enough, or does it get lost in the noise?
2. Is 10 recipes enough for a vertical slice, or would 6 feel too thin / 15 too overwhelming?
3. Any idle games on the front page right now that are doing something I should study?
4. Would you play a "Grow a" format game if it wasn't about plants/animals/food?
5. Honest take: is the educational angle a strength (widens audience) or a weakness (makes it feel like homework)?

Thanks in advance — I'll reply to every comment.
